Anthropic, I Need to Apologize

I may have spoken too soon

Published: June 1st, 2026

A little over a month ago I wrote an article called Why I Don't Like Anthropic Right Now where I complained about token limits on the Claude Pro plan shrinking to the point where I was burning through my weekly allocation just debugging code. I stand by some of what I said, but I also think I was being unfair — and I want to correct that.

Contents:

  • What changed
  • What I got wrong
  • What still stands

What Changed

Since that article I've been using Claude Code Max and the experience is genuinely different. Not just slightly better — actually different. I've been using it to build out my entire portfolio website over the past few days: adding a chatbot with a Cloudflare Worker backend, a hamburger nav menu, a contact form, a client work page, download sections for ONNX models, and more. In a couple of sessions I shipped more working features than I would have in a week of writing it myself.

And it didn't just write code blindly. It found broken hardcoded colors from an old theme, caught a CORS issue before it became a problem, tested the live worker endpoint and switched models when the first one was rate-limited. That's not a token dispenser — that's actually useful.

The other thing that changed is I stopped fighting the tool. My previous article was partly frustration from trying to use Claude Code like a search engine — short prompts, lots of back and forth, burning tokens on clarifications. When you give it full context and let it run, the token usage per actual result is much more reasonable.

What I Got Wrong

I think my main mistake was comparing the Pro plan to what it was in January 2026 and feeling cheated by the difference. That's a fair frustration, but I framed it as Anthropic being greedy when the more honest framing is: I was trying to use a professional tool on a consumer plan and hitting the ceiling.

I also recommended training a local model as a replacement. I still think local models are worth exploring [Qwen2.5 Coder is genuinely good for smaller tasks], but recommending them as a replacement for Claude Code was overblown. The capability gap is real. A 7B model running on a laptop is not a substitute for what Claude Code can do — they solve different problems.

I was also unfair to Anthropic as a company. The token limits exist because inference is expensive and they're scaling fast. I don't have to like the limits, but calling it greed without acknowledging that context wasn't honest.

What Still Stands

I still think the Pro plan limits are too tight for anyone using Claude Code seriously. If you're a student or hobbyist hitting the ceiling trying to debug real projects, that's a real problem and I don't want to pretend it isn't. The Max plan is not in everyone's budget.

And I still think the alternatives are worth knowing about — Gemini API, Deepseek, local Ollama setups. Not as replacements for Claude, but as tools in the arsenal for when you're out of tokens or working offline.

But the overall verdict I came to in that article — that Anthropic had basically become unreliable and I should look elsewhere — that was wrong. I'm glad I didn't act on it.
