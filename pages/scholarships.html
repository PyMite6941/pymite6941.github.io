Federal Cybersecurity Scholarships

The scholarship-for-service programs I'm working toward — what each one asks for, what it commits you to, and why my college list is built around them.

I'm a U.S. citizen planning to major in cybersecurity, and the federal government funds that field more directly than almost any other. These programs are not ordinary scholarships — every one of them is a scholarship for service: the government pays for your degree, you work summers at the sponsoring agency, and you owe them a defined term of employment after graduation. That trade is the point, and it lines up with the career I want.

This page is a working record of the programs I'm targeting and where each stands. Requirements and application windows are set by the agencies and change year to year, so the linked official page is always the authority over anything summarised here.

Why my college list is already a scholarship decision

Two of these programs — CyberCorps SFS and DoD CySP — are not things you win as a high school senior. You apply for them once you are already enrolled, and only if your university participates. That means choosing a school is choosing which of these doors stay open, three years before you ever fill in the application.

So I checked every school on my list against the official CyberCorps participating-institutions directory rather than assuming:

Participate in CyberCorps SFS

George Mason University, UMBC, University of Maryland College Park, and Rochester Institute of Technology are all listed participating institutions. RIT additionally holds the NSA/DHS triple designation as a Center of Academic Excellence in Cyber Operations, Research, and Defense.

Do not participate

Drexel University, Milwaukee School of Engineering, and Clark University are not on the participating-institutions list. That does not make them weak programs — it means the SFS route specifically would not be available there.

What these programs actually ask of you

It is worth being plain about the parts that are easy to skip past in a scholarship listing:

They are employment, not gifts. Accepting one means signing a continued-service agreement. Leaving early generally means repaying the government.

They require a security clearance. Every program here runs a full background investigation, and some add a medical review and a polygraph. That process is thorough and slow, and it starts long before the first tuition payment.

Some are needs-based. The CIA program in particular is explicitly a financial-need initiative with a household income ceiling, so academic record alone does not determine eligibility.

Availability changes. These programs open and close cycle to cycle, and an agency can pause intake for a year without much notice. Anything on this page should be confirmed against the official source before you rely on it.
